# ÉPOCA Operating and Go-to-Market Strategy

**Status:** Approved strategic direction; professional activation review still required  
**Decision date:** 2026-08-25  
**Review date:** Before either seller accepts an ÉPOCA order, and at least annually after launch  
**Applies to:** Business structure, inventory ownership, seller/payment routing, market entry, and buyer acquisition

## Purpose and legal boundary

This document records the recommended real-world operating model for ÉPOCA. It is the bridge between the brand, the two warehouses, and the company/payment setup.

It is a business strategy, not tailored legal or tax advice. Before activation, a German tax adviser familiar with second-hand e-commerce, a German e-commerce lawyer, and a Georgian accountant or tax lawyer must review the marked decisions. Where their written advice conflicts with this document, record the approved change here and reconcile the product specification before implementation.

ÉPOCA will use lawful tax planning: a simple structure, legitimate deductions, eligible VAT schemes, and documented cross-border service fees. It will not hide sales, stock, cash purchases, ownership, or profits. A Georgian company does not erase German obligations created by stock, people, a warehouse, or sales activity in Germany.

## Executive decision

ÉPOCA should operate as **one brand with two clearly identified sellers**, not as one Georgian seller pretending that all activity occurs in Georgia.

| Inventory and customer route                                    | Seller of record                                                                     | Stock location | Customer payment                                      | Fulfilment and return location                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| German-sourced carpet sold in Germany or the EU                 | German partner's registered German sole proprietorship                               | Germany        | German seller's approved merchant account and bank    | Germany                                                     |
| Georgian-sourced carpet sold in Georgia                         | Georgian limited liability company (LLC/LTD; use the exact registered form publicly) | Georgia        | Georgian company's approved merchant account and bank | Georgia                                                     |
| Georgian-sourced carpet sold to a selected non-EU market        | Georgian company, only after destination activation                                  | Georgia        | Georgian company's merchant account                   | Georgia, with destination-specific customs and return terms |
| Georgian carpet deliberately stocked for regular EU retail sale | German seller after a documented export/import or wholesale transfer                 | Germany        | German seller's merchant account                      | Germany                                                     |

“Seller of record” means the legal business named on the offer, checkout, payment, invoice, customer rights, refund, and tax record.

The Georgian company may own the ÉPOCA trademark, domain, software, content, and central brand operations if the registrations and contracts support that ownership. It may invoice the German seller for genuine platform, brand-licensing, content, or marketing services at a defensible market-based price. The fee must follow a written agreement, real work, normal invoices, and the correct VAT/tax treatment. It cannot be an arbitrary transfer that removes all German profit.

### Why this is the recommended model

- It matches the facts: half the goods are found, bought, stored, marketed, and shipped from Germany.
- It keeps German operations inexpensive by starting with a sole proprietorship rather than an unnecessary corporation.
- It lets the Georgian company receive Georgian retail revenue and legitimate business-to-business platform/service revenue.
- It gives customers one ÉPOCA experience while showing the correct contracting seller.
- It avoids making the platform an uncontrolled multi-vendor marketplace at launch.

## Options considered

### Option A — one brand, two seller-specific operations — recommended

Use a German sole proprietorship for German/EU stock and the Georgian company for Georgian stock. Keep separate merchant accounts, accounting, invoice sequences, seller policies, and stock ownership. A shared domain is possible only when every product and checkout clearly routes to one seller.

This is the simplest credible launch model.

### Option B — Georgian company sells German warehouse stock directly — later, not the starting model

This is possible only with German professional advice and likely German registrations. German-held inventory can create German VAT duties even when the company was incorporated in Georgia. The warehouse, local purchasing, partner authority, and continuous operations can also create a German branch or permanent-establishment risk.

If this route is ever chosen, assume it requires a German VAT registration, a German branch/permanent-establishment assessment, local accounting support, a suitable merchant account, and seller disclosures before taking the first order. Treat any tax saving as unproven until an adviser models the full cost.

### Option C — Georgian company is a marketplace collecting both sellers' money — reject for launch

Collecting German seller proceeds into Georgia and later paying the partner changes ÉPOCA from a shop into a marketplace or payment intermediary. That adds seller onboarding, payment splitting, client-money/payment-services questions, reporting, refunds, liability allocation, and more complex consumer disclosures.

Do not build or operate this model without a new specification and specialist legal/payment advice.

## How money and contracts should flow

### German-stock order

1. The German sole proprietor owns or can prove authority to sell the item.
2. The product page identifies the German seller.
3. The buyer enters a seller-specific German cart and checkout.
4. The payment provider settles the retail payment to the German seller.
5. The German seller issues the invoice, fulfils the item, handles the withdrawal/return, and records tax.
6. The German seller pays any documented ÉPOCA platform or licence fee to the Georgian company under the business-to-business agreement.

### Georgian-stock order

1. The Georgian company owns the item and its acquisition record.
2. The product page identifies the Georgian seller and origin of dispatch.
3. The payment provider settles the retail payment to the Georgian company.
4. The Georgian company issues the invoice, exports where required, fulfils the item, handles the return, and records tax.

### Cart rule

Do not allow one checkout to contain products from both sellers. Either keep market catalogues separate or ask the buyer to place two clearly disclosed orders. A mixed cart creates unnecessary payment, invoice, delivery, cancellation, and liability complexity.

## Inventory and cash-purchase rules

Cash purchasing is not automatically prohibited. Undocumented commercial purchasing is the problem.

For every item acquired for resale, create a sequential purchase record on the day of purchase containing:

- acquisition ID, date, time, place, currency, and cash amount;
- description, dimensions, condition, identifying marks, and photographs;
- seller name and address, plus identity verification where required and proportionate;
- seller declaration that they own the item and can transfer it;
- buyer legal entity, signatures, and a receipt or private purchase agreement;
- provenance statements separated into verified, seller-reported, and unknown facts;
- cashbook entry and link to the product/inventory record;
- restoration, cleaning, transport, storage, and sale records.

The German seller must own and record German-acquired stock. The Georgian company must not book a German cash purchase as its own unless it was genuinely the buyer and advisers have approved the German consequences.

For high-value cash or linked transactions, apply the identity, anti-money-laundering, and suspicious-transaction checks required for goods traders. Do not split payments to avoid a threshold.

### Existing German activity transition

Before moving the partner's current operation into ÉPOCA:

1. Export and preserve eBay listings, orders, payouts, messages needed for transactions, and fees.
2. Count and photograph warehouse stock; separate personal possessions from goods bought for resale.
3. Gather bank, PayPal/payment, courier, restoration, rent, and cash-purchase evidence.
4. Do not destroy records, invent suppliers, or create backdated receipts.
5. Give the full record to a German tax adviser and, if they identify past exposure, a German tax lawyer before filing corrections or contacting an authority.
6. Convert the eBay account to the correct commercial status and seller identity before future ÉPOCA/eBay commercial sales.

The German Federal Fiscal Court treats planned purchasing for resale as a strong sign of commercial activity, and eBay itself says an account should be commercial when items were bought for resale. Platform reporting thresholds are reporting rules, not tax-free allowances.

## Seller and tax activation checklist

### German seller

- Register the trade and sole proprietorship; obtain the required tax numbers and business bank/payment setup.
- Have an adviser assess income tax, trade tax, VAT registration, and whether the small-business VAT rule is beneficial and available.
- Assess the second-hand margin VAT scheme item by item. It can be valuable where goods are acquired from private persons, but its purchase evidence, invoicing, exports, and EU distance-sale treatment require professional setup.
- Register packaging obligations before dispatching packaged goods and assess destination-country extended producer responsibility obligations.
- Prepare German/EU-compliant legal notice, seller identity, price/tax display, withdrawal form, return terms, legal guarantee handling, privacy, cookies, and marketing consent.
- Review each product and online offer under the EU General Product Safety Regulation, including traceability and safety/warning information where applicable.
- Assess One Stop Shop and destination VAT before opening additional EU countries. Do not apply the general EUR 10,000 distance-sales rule blindly to stock under a second-hand special scheme.
- Verify warehouse use, insurance, fire/security requirements, goods-in-transit insurance, and customer appointment rules.

### Georgian company

- Register the company, beneficial owners, directors, tax status, bank account, accounting policy, and exact public legal name.
- Confirm where management and platform work actually occur; do not assume Georgian incorporation alone decides all tax residence or permanent-establishment questions.
- Obtain written payment-provider approval for products, markets, currencies, refunds, chargebacks, and settlement.
- Prepare compliant invoices, export/customs evidence, customer policies, privacy terms, and records retention.
- Sign the German–Georgian intellectual-property/platform services agreement and validate its price and VAT treatment.
- Review brand ownership and trademark registration before licensing the ÉPOCA name.

### Activate a destination only when all are true

- The correct seller, tax display, invoice, and consumer terms have been approved.
- The payment provider permits the seller, goods, currency, and destination.
- A carrier accepts the dimensions/value and insurance covers loss and damage.
- Shipping price, delivery estimate, duties/importer responsibility, and bulky-return cost can be stated honestly.
- Product-safety, packaging, privacy, sanctions, and restricted-party checks are complete where applicable.
- Customer support can operate in the promised language.

## Payment strategy

Use separate merchant accounts because the money must settle to the business that sold the item.

### Georgian seller

Start the commercial comparison with TBC E-Commerce and Bank of Georgia. Confirm international card acceptance, GEL/EUR/USD enablement, 3-D Secure, refunds, chargebacks, payout timing, reserves, hosted-checkout languages, and cross-border customer acceptance in writing. The product specification currently chooses TBC as the first technical adapter, but this is not merchant approval.

### German seller

Compare providers available to the registered German sole proprietor only after the seller and VAT setup are complete. Evaluate card and wallet coverage, PayPal if commercially useful, payout holds, chargeback handling, refund costs, bulky-goods risk, and supported countries. Do not choose a provider only because integration looks easy.

### Payment controls

- Payment account legal name must match the seller shown to the buyer.
- Keep separate settlement accounts and reconciliation ledgers.
- Store provider tokens and transaction references, never raw card details.
- Verify payment callbacks and reconcile uncertain states before shipment.
- Refund through the original seller and method where supported.
- Never use a personal account or the partner's private eBay identity for new commercial ÉPOCA sales.

## Market-entry sequence

### Priority 1 — Germany

Germany should be the first scalable market for German-held stock because the inventory, operator, fulfilment, returns, and existing eBay learning are already there. Launch German-language product pages, German seller details, domestic delivery, and a controlled catalogue first.

### Priority 2 — Georgia

Georgia should launch as the brand's home and trust-building market for Georgian-held inventory. Use Georgian first, with English and Russian as language support where useful. Focus on Tbilisi buyers, design professionals, hospitality, and destination projects before promising broad regional delivery.

### Priority 3 — Austria

Open Austria only after Germany has produced reliable packaging, damage, delivery-time, return, and support data. German-language content transfers well, but Austrian consumer, VAT, packaging, and delivery settings still need explicit activation.

### Priority 4 — selected EU markets

Test one market at a time based on qualified traffic, designer demand, carrier reliability, and contribution margin. English can support international discovery, but it must not automatically make every country purchasable.

### Russian language is a segment, not the initial Russia market

Use Russian pages and ads for Russian-speaking customers in Georgia, Germany, and other activated markets. Do not target or accept orders into Russia merely because the interface is translated; payments, sanctions, logistics, customs, and platform policies need a separate current review.

## Buyer-acquisition strategy

ÉPOCA sells one-of-a-kind, visual, considered purchases. The growth system should combine **high purchase intent** (people already searching for a carpet), **visual discovery** (people forming a room idea), and **professional referrals** (designers who repeatedly source for clients).

Marketplaces are additional sales channels, not a method for secretly redirecting marketplace buyers to ÉPOCA. eBay prohibits sharing contact details before a purchase or using its buyer information for off-platform sales. 1stDibs also prohibits off-platform offers arising from its service. Build ÉPOCA's own consented audience independently.

### What ÉPOCA should publish

Every piece of content should answer a buying question:

- **Scale:** the carpet in a real room, a simple floor plan, dimensions, and person/furniture reference.
- **Truthful condition:** front, back, edges, pile, repairs, stains, and colour variation in neutral light.
- **Origin and technique:** only verified facts; visibly label seller-reported or unknown details.
- **Styling:** one carpet in two or three room directions without changing its real colour.
- **Education:** how to choose a size, understand hand-knotting, care for wool, and plan a bulky return.
- **The hunt:** show how pieces are found and evaluated without exposing private sellers or inventing stories.
- **New arrival and sold archive:** create a weekly rhythm and preserve useful editorial/SEO history without implying a sold item is available.
- **Founder and service:** who selects the pieces, where each seller ships from, how questions and damage claims are handled.

For German search, build natural pages around real attributes such as `Vintage Teppich`, `handgeknüpfter Teppich`, `Kelimteppich`, `Orientteppich gebraucht`, exact dimensions, colour, material, and verified origin. Do not keyword-stuff or translate unverified provenance into certainty.

## First 90 days

### Days 1–14 — make the offer trustworthy

- Finish the German transition review and Georgian company/payment onboarding.
- Select 20–30 launch pieces with complete ownership, condition, dimensions, margin, shipping, and return data.
- Create consistent photography: full view, room view, back, edges, pile, damage, and scale.
- Make seller identity, dispatch country, total price, delivery, returns, and support obvious on every product.
- Install consent-aware measurement, Merchant Center feed, product structured data, Pinterest catalogue, and email opt-in.

### Days 15–30 — soft launch

- Invite a small group of real buyers and five to ten design professionals to test comprehension and trust.
- Publish three useful editorial pieces: sizing, condition/quality evaluation, and care/delivery.
- Put the strongest German products on the compliant eBay account and ÉPOCA site with synchronized availability.
- Start a weekly new-arrivals email only for people who explicitly subscribed.
- Fix the reasons people hesitate before buying traffic.

### Days 31–60 — establish repeatable traffic

- Submit Google free listings and publish Pinterest/Instagram content from the catalogue.
- Contact a small, researched list of designers each week with relevant pieces, not a mass message.
- Test a simple trade offer: sourcing assistance, short holds, project invoice, and dependable delivery; do not lead with permanent discounts.
- Review search terms, saved products, enquiries, shipping quotes, and sold categories weekly.

### Days 61–90 — controlled paid tests

- Run small Google Shopping/Search tests on the products and queries that already show intent.
- Retarget consented site visitors only after privacy controls work correctly.
- Test three creative angles: room transformation, exact-size solution, and one-of-one provenance/condition transparency.
- Apply to PAMONO only if stock quality, operations, and channel economics are ready.
- Decide whether Austria is ready using real German fulfilment and return evidence.

## Budget and measurement

Start with free listings, existing marketplace demand, organic content, and founder-led trade outreach. Paid advertising should amplify a proven product/market combination, not diagnose a broken checkout.

Calculate the maximum acquisition cost per order from the actual item economics:

```text
contribution margin = selling price
                    - acquisition cost
                    - restoration and inbound transport
                    - VAT/tax directly attributable to the sale
                    - payment and marketplace fees
                    - packaging and outbound shipping subsidy
                    - expected return, damage, and chargeback cost

maximum customer-acquisition cost = contribution margin × approved marketing share
```

Choose the marketing share with the accountant and cash-flow plan. Never optimize ads to revenue while ignoring VAT, returns, marketplace fees, or inventory cost.

Track at minimum:

- contribution margin by item, seller, destination, and channel;
- qualified product visits and search terms;
- product-view-to-save/email/enquiry rate;
- add-to-cart, checkout-start, payment-completion, and abandonment reasons;
- customer-acquisition cost and payback by channel;
- days to sell and ageing inventory;
- delivery delay, damage, return, refund, and chargeback rates;
- repeat/referral and trade-customer revenue;
- stock mismatches across ÉPOCA, eBay, and curated marketplaces.

Followers and impressions are supporting signals, not success. The primary launch proof is profitable, accurately fulfilled orders with low avoidable returns.

## Promotion rules and avoid list

- Do not buy followers, reviews, email lists, or fake press coverage.
- Do not import eBay buyer contact data into ÉPOCA marketing.
- Do not advertise “worldwide shipping,” free returns, rarity, age, origin, or restoration claims unless verified.
- Do not run broad international ads before the destination is operationally activated.
- Do not make permanent discounts the brand's main reason to buy.
- Do not use tracking or behavioural advertising cookies before the required consent.
- Clearly identify paid partnerships, promotional conditions, seller identity, and material product limitations.
- Keep one source of truth for availability so a unique item is not sold twice.

## Product and specification impact

The approved master build goal and `specs/001-build-production-shop/` currently assume one Georgian merchant, one TBC-oriented payment adapter, worldwide selling from Georgia, and no multi-vendor behavior. This strategy is a later business decision and materially changes those assumptions.

Before implementing checkout, payments, invoices, refunds, tax, seller policies, or marketplace feeds, run a specification clarification and update the plan/tasks. The smallest safe product model is:

- one ÉPOCA brand and domain;
- exactly one legal seller per product and order;
- seller-specific catalogue eligibility, cart, checkout, merchant account, invoice sequence, policy set, fulfilment, and return address;
- no mixed-seller cart;
- no platform-held seller funds or automated partner payout;
- immutable order snapshot of seller identity and commercial terms;
- fields for `seller_entity`, `inventory_owner`, `warehouse`, `dispatch_country`, `tax_scheme`, `merchant_account`, `invoice_series`, `return_address`, and `market_eligibility`;
- synchronized unique-item availability across owned and third-party channels.

This can remain a single-brand shop if the two seller operations are deliberately separated. If the business later adds unrelated sellers, commissions, onboarding, or split payouts, treat it as a new marketplace product with a new legal and technical programme.

## Decisions that require named professional approval

Record the adviser, date, jurisdiction, answer, and evidence link for each:

1. German treatment of the partner's existing eBay/resale activity and transition inventory.
2. German entity/trade registration, VAT choice, second-hand margin scheme, trade tax, and EU distance-sales treatment.
3. Whether the Georgian company's platform/brand activity creates German tax presence, and the arm's-length intercompany/service fee.
4. Ownership and licensing of the ÉPOCA name, domain, software, photographs, and customer data.
5. German/EU consumer, product-safety, packaging, privacy, advertising, and email/cookie wording.
6. Georgian company tax, management location, exports, invoices, data transfers, and payment eligibility.
7. Merchant account ownership, reserves, supported countries/currencies, chargebacks, and refund operations for each seller.
